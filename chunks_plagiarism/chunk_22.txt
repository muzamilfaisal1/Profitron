The second was a Diebold-Mariano test on the predictive accuracy of the LSTM against the predictive accuracy of an ARIMA baseline on the same held-out window. The Diebold-Mariano test is the standard statistical procedure for comparing the predictive accuracy of two competing forecast models on the same time series. The test again rejected the null hypothesis of equal accuracy at a p-value below 0.01, supporting the claim that the LSTM extracts information that the ARIMA baseline does not.

The third was a Monte Carlo permutation test on the trade sequence, in which we randomly permuted the trade entries and exits across the evaluation window and computed the resulting returns under the same risk-management framework. The empirical Sharpe ratio under the original trade sequence sat above the ninety-ninth percentile of the permutation distribution, which gives us a non-parametric basis for rejecting the null that the trades were timed randomly.

5.13 Ablation Studies

To assess the contribution of each design decision, we performed ablation studies in which individual components were removed or replaced and the resulting system was re-evaluated on the held-out window. Four ablations are worth reporting.

The first ablation removed the volatility-scaled stop-loss in favour of a fixed-percentage stop. The realised Sharpe ratio dropped by approximately 0.4 and the maximum drawdown nearly doubled, confirming the importance of volatility scaling. The second ablation removed the regime-detection gate, allowing the agent to trade through range-bound periods. The realised return dropped by approximately two percentage points over the six-month window, consistent with the regime-conditional analysis. The third ablation replaced the LSTM with a logistic-regression baseline trained on the same indicator vector. The classification accuracy dropped from approximately seventy-two percent to approximately sixty-three percent, and the realised Sharpe dropped from approximately 1.5 to approximately 0.8, confirming that the LSTM contributes meaningful predictive structure beyond what a linear model captures. The fourth ablation removed the dropout regularisation. Training-set accuracy rose, validation-set accuracy fell, and live performance dropped sharply, illustrating the standard overfitting pathology that dropout is intended to prevent.

5.14 User Adoption Metrics

Beyond the trading-performance metrics, we tracked behavioural metrics on the pilot cohort. The notification system delivered alerts with above ninety-eight percent reliability, and users responded to commands within a median of approximately one and a half seconds. Approximately three quarters of pilot users created at least one custom strategy, suggesting that the programmable surface was being exercised rather than tolerated. The most popular custom strategy across users was a moving-average crossover with a confirming RSI signal, which is consistent with the broader observation that simple, interpretable strategies are preferred over opaque ones in retail settings.

Manual overrides — users intervening to close a position early or to force a trade — reduced average performance by approximately four percent, with the impact concentrated almost entirely in volatile sessions. After the introduction of the cool-down feature that requires confirmation for overrides during high-volatility periods, the override-related performance drag fell to approximately one and a half percent, an improvement we attribute to the brief friction the cool-down imposes between an impulse and an action.

5.15 Per-Asset Generalisation

The model was trained primarily on ETH/USDT minute-scale data, but we evaluated its zero-shot performance on a basket of other major cryptocurrencies to assess generalisation. The cross-asset evaluation, summarised earlier in the paper through the per-asset accuracy figure, shows that the model retains a meaningful directional edge on closely related assets — BTC, BNB, SOL — but its accuracy degrades on smaller-cap assets whose microstructure differs more substantially. The implication for a production deployment is that the model can be applied to a portfolio of related assets without per-asset retraining, but that wider asset coverage requires either separate models or a multi-asset training regime that we have not yet attempted.

5.16 Live vs. Backtest Performance Gap

A consistent observation throughout the deployment was that live performance ran approximately one percentage point per month below the corresponding backtest performance over the same window. The size of the gap is not unusual in systematic trading research and is usually attributed to a combination of slippage, latency, and subtle differences in the data feed between backtest and live deployment. We performed a decomposition analysis to attribute the gap to its specific causes.

Slippage accounted for approximately fifty percent of the gap. The backtest assumed fills at the midpoint of the bid-ask spread, but live fills occurred at the prevailing market price after a small but non-zero delay between the decision and the order arrival at the exchange. Latency-induced price drift accounted for approximately thirty percent. The price moved during the time between the decision and the fill in a direction that was, on average, slightly adverse to the trade — a manifestation of the well-known phenomenon that orders submitted in response to short-term signals tend to arrive after the signal has partially reflected itself in price.

Data-feed differences accounted for the remaining twenty percent. The historical data we used in backtest came from a different snapshot than the live data we observed during deployment, and although both sources nominally represented the same exchange, small discrepancies in tick aggregation and timestamp resolution introduced systematic biases. The decomposition is informative because it points to where future engineering effort would most usefully be directed: improving slippage handling through smarter order types, reducing latency through more aggressive use of WebSocket connections, and aligning the backtest data feed more carefully with the production data feed.
